Most agents already know they should follow up with leads faster. The harder part is doing it consistently, in a voice that doesn't sound like a form letter, without spending twenty minutes drafting each one by hand. These templates are built to be adapted, not used verbatim — replace the bracketed details with the actual lead's information every time.

## Why timing matters more than wording

Responding to a new lead within the first five minutes can raise conversion likelihood by roughly 30-50% compared to slower response times, and most agents take considerably longer than that. A well-worded email sent six hours later will almost always underperform a merely decent email sent immediately. If you take one thing from this article, it's this: use these prompts to draft fast, not to draft perfectly.

## Five prompt templates by scenario

**1. Immediate response to a new quote request**

> "Write a short, warm email responding to a new lead who just requested an auto insurance quote through our website. Thank them for reaching out, confirm we received their request, tell them a licensed agent will call within [timeframe], and ask one clarifying question: [insert specific question, e.g., current insurance expiration date]. Keep it under 100 words. Do not mention price or savings."

**2. Re-engaging a lead who went quiet**

> "Write a brief follow-up email to a lead who requested a homeowners quote [X] days ago and hasn't responded to two prior emails. Acknowledge they're likely busy, offer one easy next step (a specific time to call, or a one-question reply), and keep the tone low-pressure, not pushy. Under 80 words."

**3. Post-quote follow-up when the client hasn't decided**

> "Write a follow-up email for a client who received a life insurance quote [X] days ago and hasn't responded. Remind them of the quote without restating every number. Ask if they have questions about the coverage or the process, not just whether they're ready to buy. Under 100 words."

**4. Renewal reminder with a cross-sell opening**

> "Write a renewal reminder email for a client whose auto policy renews in [X] days. Confirm the renewal is happening automatically (or requires action, if applicable), thank them for their continued business, and mention — briefly, without pressure — that we also handle [line of business, e.g., renters insurance] in case that's useful to review. Under 90 words."

**5. Responding to a lead who compared prices elsewhere**

> "Write a response to a lead who mentioned they got a lower quote from another company. Don't be defensive or discount the other quote. Ask what specifically they're comparing (coverage limits, deductible, or price alone) and offer to review both quotes together. Keep it curious and helpful in tone, not competitive. Under 100 words."

## Compliance guardrails for AI-written client communication

Speed and tone matter, but what the email actually claims matters more, and this is where AI-generated drafts need a human check before sending — every time, not occasionally.

**Never let a draft include:**
- Guaranteed savings ("you'll definitely save money by switching")
- Implied approval before underwriting is complete ("you're approved for this rate")
- Specific coverage promises that haven't been confirmed against the actual quote or policy
- Language that could be read as advice about whether to buy, rather than information about what's available, if your state distinguishes between the two for licensing purposes

**Check your state's requirements** on required disclosures in marketing and client communication — these vary by state and by line of business, and some states have specific rules about electronic communication with prospective policyholders. This content is educational, not legal or compliance advice; confirm current requirements with your state Department of Insurance or your agency's compliance resource before using AI-generated communication at scale.

A reasonable practice for most small agencies: use AI to draft the first version fast, then have a licensed person read it before it goes out — not to rewrite it entirely, but to check specifically for the claims above. This preserves the speed advantage without giving up the compliance check that matters.
